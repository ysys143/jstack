# Narrative research report

## Selection and format boundary

`SKILL.md` selects this route. It turns supplied findings, notes, or sources
into a narrative report, or revises such a report for readers. It is a
lightweight route, not a sixth genre mode. A request that first needs a new
investigation without supplied material belongs to the `research` skill; use
this reference when its findings are written up.

The task is to organize collected items in the order a reader understands them.
Make the prose natural and keep the information exact at the same time. Cutting
sentences short or deleting list markers does not finish a report edit.

Two report forms exist, and they take different rules:

| Form | Content | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Itemized briefing | Meeting document, slide, status or plan report in the itemized public-report form, any report requested as `개조식` or with `□ ○ -` | `references/direct.md`, itemized writing |
| Narrative report | Findings of research, investigation, analysis, or technical study, written in paragraphs | This reference |

Choose the form from the content when the request does not name it. Findings
of an investigation or analysis take the narrative form. Meeting, status, or
plan material takes the itemized form. When the content fits both, ask once.
When the user names a decision memo, a one-page summary, or a conclusion-first
layout, follow that structure and do not force the opening described below. Do
not add recommendations, implementation plans, or checklists that the user did
not ask for.

Execution stays lightweight. Do not load the taxonomies, playbooks, or
specialist pipelines, and do not create a workspace, run scripts, call a
reviewer, assign scores, or label the result. The ASD-STE100 baseline does not
govern report structure: do not split descriptive paragraphs into
one-proposition sentences or turn evidence into vertical lists to satisfy it.

## Fix the inventory before writing

When a source and an earlier edit both exist, compare their introductions,
chapter order, representative body paragraphs, and conclusions. For each
statement, identify who does what, under which condition, and how far it was
confirmed. Keep each number with its unit, its counting basis, and its source.
Do not merge names, subjects of analysis, data dates, or review scopes that the
source keeps apart merely because one name reads more easily. When related
material is missing, do not infer it. Edit only what the supplied material
supports.

## Open with the subject, not the verdict

The first paragraph presents a task or phenomenon the reader already
recognizes: who does what, and with which material or means. It then defines
the report's subject by the role it plays in that task and states which part of
the wider field the report covers. **Do not judge the subject's maturity,
quality, or limits before explaining what it is.**

The next paragraph explains how the subjects are composed and how they relate.
Use representative cases only as far as they explain that relation. Then state
the purpose and review scope, followed by the nature and limits of the material
needed to interpret the findings. State those limits in prose where they bear
on interpretation, instead of a list of reading conventions placed before the
content; a methods or limitations chapter remains appropriate when the report
needs one. This order is a default flow, not a fixed paragraph count. Cut
background the reader already knows, but keep the explanation needed to
understand technical terms and the relation between the compared subjects.

The introduction is not a compressed list of conclusions. Do not open with a
bold verdict and spend the rest defending it, and do not fill space with the
general importance of the field. Renaming a verdict-style heading to a neutral
overview heading is not enough; change what the sentences do. Present the
overall judgment after the cases and evidence. When the user asked for a
separate summary, keep it apart from the introduction.

## Order the body as the reader understands it

Explain the concept and the scope of work first, then the composition of the
compared subjects, then individual cases, then the issues they share, and then
the overall assessment. Derive chapter titles from the topic; do not reuse a
template's titles. Do not repeat what was already explained at the start and
end of every chapter.

For each case, first explain what the subject is and what role it plays, then
how it works and under which conditions. When a fact from a case description
reappears in a comparison chapter, explain which commonality or difference it
supports instead of listing it again. Move inventory statistics, detailed
versions, long numeric tables, and source lists to an appendix when they break
the argument. Keep in the body any limiting condition that changes the meaning
of a conclusion.

## Rebuild paragraphs from items

Group the note items by topic and logical role, then rewrite each group as one
paragraph. The first sentence names what the paragraph explains. The following
sentences cover action and condition, a contrasting case, and interpretive
limits, in whatever order the content needs. Do not force every paragraph into
a template such as claim, three examples, conclusion. **Sentences sharing one
subject is not enough; each sentence must give the reader a reason to read the
next.**

When listed items describe mechanisms, procedures, or actors that apply under
different conditions, explain which applies when instead of merely counting
them. Do not create causal links the source lacks by inserting connectives.
Split a long sentence at a meaning boundary, but never cut off a condition or
exception so that it reads as a separate general fact.

## Write sentences with a concrete actor and action

The subject of a sentence is the entity that performs the action. An entity
that supplies or funds something, an entity that carries out an operation, and
a person who approves it are different actors; do not put them under one
subject. Prefer who reads, creates, or changes what over abstract nouns such as
`기능 확장`, `고도화`, or `효율성 제고`. Turn a noun-form predicate into a
complete predicate when that makes the meaning clearer.

Do not repeat `문서는 적는다`, `확인한 사실은 다음과 같다`, or `셋으로 나뉜다`
sentence after sentence. Group explanation drawn from one source at paragraph
level, and keep a condition or uncertainty on the claim it belongs to. When a
party's own claim must stay distinct from a verified result, name the source as
the subject, as in `문서는 설명한다`. Do not shorten attributions by turning a
documented claim into an unconditional statement about what actually happens.

Write Korean body text as complete sentences, in the `한다` style unless the
user or the source sets another register, without exclamation or exhortation.
A paragraph usually holds three to seven sentences; do not add background or a
closing line to reach that range. Do not break lines inside a paragraph for
emphasis, and mark names and key terms only where needed. When a demonstrative
leaves unclear which item it means, repeat the name.

## Keep names and units of comparison

**Different levels of one hierarchy are different units of comparison**: an
organization and what it supplies, a program and a measure inside it, a system
and one of its components. Name the level that actually performs the action
being described. Do not attribute every property of a whole to one part inside
it, or a part's property to the whole. Do not coin a proper name the material
does not contain.

When comparing different subjects, first state the task they share. Do not
present different attributes, such as ownership, delivery form, and role, as
one exclusive classification. Do not restate a count of mixed kinds as a count
of one kind, or a count of connections as a count of the things connected.
When a change of term alters an inclusion relation or a counting unit, treat it
as a change of meaning, not a style edit.

## Readability does not widen the evidence

Distinguish `읽은 범위에서 찾지 못했다` from `없다`. Do not generalize an
observation of part of the material to the whole subject, and do not infer a
property from the absence of one dedicated mechanism. Treat a party's
documented claims, direct observation of a fixed version or snapshot, results
of an actual test or trial, and self-reported results as different kinds of
evidence. After compressing a sentence, check that the source's conditions and
exceptions survived.

State uncertainty where it affects a judgment, and state it specifically.
Instead of ending every paragraph with `추가 검증이 필요하다`, say which item or
material was not confirmed and to what extent. Different wording across
documents is not a contradiction when the version, period, scope, or target
differs. When the material cannot settle a relation, leave it unsettled and do
not fill the gap with familiar background knowledge.

When describing results, keep what was measured in the sentence. Do not turn
one measure into a neighboring one, such as the time to one milestone into the
time to another, the cost of one unit into a total cost, or an estimate into an
observation. Do not convert a source's `더 빠르다` into a rate or a multiple. A
number whose definition cannot be confirmed is not a basis for quantitative
comparison, however much it would simplify the explanation.

## Separate editing from new research

When only a writing edit is requested, edit from the supplied material and do
not write as if a new investigation took place. When the user also asks to
check the report's facts or to research further, consult the needed sources,
but keep newly confirmed content distinct from records retained from the
source. Checking facts here concerns the report's content; it is not the
edit-fidelity verification route that `SKILL.md` selects. Do not claim to have
read material that was not read or to have run checks that were not run.

## Apply each change to the whole report

After correcting a name or a judgment, check whether the same claim remains in
the title, introduction, body, comparison tables, conclusion, and appendices.
Reading only the first sentence of each paragraph in order should reveal how
the argument develops. Then compare the subject, action, condition, and
evidence of every sentence with the source to find meaning that changed during
editing. **Naturalness comes from making clear which fact a condition attaches
to, not from deleting conditions.**

Do not insert an assessment such as `읽기 쉽게 고쳤다` or a lengthy narration of
the editing process into the body. Keep scope and evidence limits as far as
interpretation needs them. Keep the editing process and change list in a
separate record, and only when requested. The conclusion states observations
drawn from the preceding evidence. It adds no claim absent from the body, and
no recommendation unless the user asked for one. The final format follows the
report or file format the user specified.

## Revision patterns

Each pattern names what changes between a weak draft and a reader-facing
report. Apply the pattern, not any wording, to the topic at hand.

1. **Verdict-first opening to task-first opening.** The draft's first sentence
   judges the subject and the rest defends that judgment. The revision first
   describes the task and material the reader knows, then defines the subject
   by its role in that task, then states the report's scope. What changes is
   the order in which information reaches the reader, not the difficulty of
   the words.
2. **Flat name list to relation between levels.** The draft lists entities from
   different levels in one enumeration as if they were peers. The revision
   names each item at its own level, states in the same sentence which entity
   it belongs to, and says that the report keeps those levels apart.
3. **Short items to a paragraph.** The draft states a subject's nature, its
   parts, and its conditions as unrelated one-line facts. The revision starts
   from what the subject is, explains how its parts differ in when or how they
   apply, and brings in related facts from other items; unrelated items go to
   other paragraphs. What changes is the selection and order of related facts,
   not the gluing of list items into sentences.
