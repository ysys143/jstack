# ASD-STE100: recommended clarity resource

## Use in Humanize

Use this reference as the recommended starting point for an editing profile,
not as a mandatory style filter. Explicit user requests and adopted project
rules take precedence over this recommendation; preserve the source meaning.
A project or personal profile can select, adapt, or exclude these principles.
Do not infer stable superiority from the two-generation document experiment.
Existing Humanize taxonomies remain separate resources, not part of STE.
A narrative research, investigation, analysis, or technical report takes its
structure and paragraphing from `references/research-report.md`; apply these
principles only inside its sentences.

For Korean and general prose, use principles mode. Read the Korean application
section below; do not apply English word counts or claim formal compliance.
For English technical text, use rule-aligned mode unless the full official
rules and controlled dictionary have actually been checked. Strict auditing
requires the official source; this summary alone cannot establish compliance.
When the user requests only the rewritten body, keep verification notes outside
that body. Never invent facts, actors, or stronger claims to satisfy a rule.

# ASD-STE100 Issue 9 operational profile

This is an original operational summary. It is not a copy of the standard or a
substitute for its controlled dictionary.

## Provenance and scope

- Owner and maintainer: ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group
  (STEMG).
- Current source checked for this profile: Issue 9, dated 2025-01-15.
- Official standard: <https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf>
- Official overview: <https://www.asd-ste100.org/about_STE.html>
- Official FAQ: <https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html>
- Official download page: <https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html>

Issue 9 contains 53 writing rules in nine sections and a controlled dictionary
of approximately 900 general words. It permits approved domain terms as
technical nouns and technical verbs. The standard targets English technical
documentation, especially procedures and descriptions. Its principles can
help other writing, but that use is not ASD-STE100 compliance.

The official PDF states that ASD owns the content and restricts unauthorized
reproduction and distribution. Link to the official copy. Do not add the PDF,
the dictionary, or copied rule text to a public package.

## Operational rule groups

Use these groups to plan and review a rewrite. For a strict audit, resolve every
question against the official Issue 9 document rather than treating this
summary as the final authority.

### 1. Words and terminology

- Use only an approved general word or a valid technical noun or technical
  verb.
- Use an approved word only with its permitted meaning, part of speech, and
  inflected form.
- Keep technical names short, recognizable, domain-approved, and consistent.
  Do not turn a technical noun into a verb or a technical verb into a noun.
- Use American English spelling unless another governing directive requires a
  different spelling.

### 2. Multi-word nouns

- Keep an ordinary multi-word noun to no more than three words.
- When an established technical name is longer, first write its full form and
  then define a clear short form or use suitable hyphenation. Do not silently
  replace a controlled product or component name.

### 3. Verbs

- Prefer simple verb forms and simple time references. Avoid chains of
  auxiliaries and ambiguous verb constructions.
- Use active voice. In descriptive text, passive voice is acceptable when the
  source does not identify the agent.
- Use a verb to state an action. Avoid converting the action into an abstract
  noun phrase.
- Treat an `-ing` form as a technical name or a modifier inside a technical
  name only when the standard permits that use.

### 4. General sentence construction

- Write complete, short sentences. Do not omit necessary words or use
  contractions merely to reduce length.
- Use vertical lists when a sentence contains a complex set of conditions,
  alternatives, or results.
- Connect related sentences with clear linking words. Use articles or
  demonstratives when they identify the intended item.

### 5. Procedures

- Limit an English procedural sentence to 20 words under the standard's word
  counting rules.
- Give one instruction per sentence, except when actions must occur at the same
  time.
- Use the imperative for an instruction. Put a condition first when the reader
  must know it before acting.
- A note gives information. Do not hide an instruction inside a note.

### 6. Descriptions

- Give information in a logical progression and repeat key terminology rather
  than varying it for style.
- Limit an English descriptive sentence to 25 words under the standard's word
  counting rules.
- Keep one topic in each paragraph and no more than six sentences in a
  paragraph.

### 7. Safety instructions

- Select the signal word from the actual risk level and the governing domain
  convention. In the ASD profile, injury or death maps to a warning, while
  equipment damage maps to a caution.
- Start with the action or condition that prevents the harm.
- State the hazard or possible result explicitly. Do not weaken or infer a
  safety consequence during rewriting.

### 8. Punctuation and counting

- Do not use a semicolon in strict STE. Split the relationship into clear
  sentences.
- Use hyphens to make directly related words clear. Use parentheses only for
  the functions permitted by the standard.
- Apply the Issue 9 counting method. Numbers with units, identifiers,
  abbreviations, quoted text, labels, proper names, parenthetical text, and
  hyphenated words have special counting treatment. A colon before a vertical
  list ends the sentence for counting.

### 9. Writing practice

- Restructure the sentence when a word-for-word substitution would be
  ungrammatical or would change meaning.
- Check each approved word in context. Avoid unapproved phrasal verbs.
- Use the same terminology and construction each time the same operation or
  item recurs.

## Audit result

Report each issue with its location, text type, rule group, consequence, and a
minimal correction. Separate these states:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `verified` | Checked against the official Issue 9 rule and dictionary entry |
| `rule-only` | Writing rule checked; controlled vocabulary not fully checked |
| `principles-only` | Transferable clarity principle applied outside strict English STE |
| `unresolved` | Domain meaning, approved term, or safety consequence needs an expert |

An automated or model review can produce findings. It does not by itself grant
certification or prove that a domain term is approved.


# Applying STE principles to Korean technical prose

Korean is outside ASD-STE100's controlled English and dictionary. Use the
status `STE-informed rewrite; not ASD-STE100 compliant`. Do not translate the
English dictionary into a Korean approved-word list or mechanically apply
English grammar restrictions.

## Transfer the clarity decisions

- Make the actor explicit when omission creates ambiguity. If the source does
  not establish the actor, preserve that uncertainty instead of inventing one.
- Put one action in each procedural sentence. In descriptive prose, split a
  sentence when multiple causes, actions, and consequences obscure their
  relationships. Keep it whole when splitting would detach a condition or
  exception from its claim or drop the relation that joins two facts.
- Put a prerequisite or condition before the dependent action.
- Use the same Korean or English technical term for the same item throughout.
  Do not alternate synonyms merely to avoid repetition.
- Prefer a direct verb to an abstract action noun followed by `수행하다`,
  `진행하다`, or `이루어지다` when the direct form preserves the meaning.
- Use active constructions when the actor is known. Retain passive or
  impersonal wording when the actor is unknown or intentionally unspecified.
- Use a vertical list for parallel requirements, choices, or risks that the
  reader checks one by one. In descriptive prose, keep the evidence for one
  point in the paragraph that says how each item bears on that point. Keep one
  topic per paragraph and use six sentences as a review signal, not as a Korean
  conformance threshold.
- For safety text, identify the risk level, state the preventive action or
  condition first, and preserve the consequence exactly.

## Do not transfer English-only mechanics

- Do not apply the 20-word and 25-word English limits to Korean eojeol counts.
  Judge whether the reader can recover actor, action, condition, and result
  without rereading.
- Do not ban Korean equivalents of English `-ing` forms, articles,
  contractions, phrasal verbs, or American spelling rules.
- Do not claim that a Korean sentence uses an approved ASD dictionary word.
- Do not turn a personal essay, event report, research report, or persuasive
  document into a maintenance procedure or an itemized list. Preserve genre and
  voice while improving technical clarity.

## Fidelity check

Compare source and candidate for facts, numbers, names, modality, negation,
conditions, causal direction, quotations, code, links, and heading scope. A
clearer sentence with a changed claim is a failed rewrite. If the requested
output is body-only, perform this check internally and return only the body.
